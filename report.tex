\begin{abstract}
We present a multi-view pipeline for tracking basketball players and the ball and for reconstructing their 3D trajectories, using three fixed, calibrated cameras installed at the Sanbapolis facility in Trento. Ground-truth bounding boxes for players, referees, and the ball were manually annotated at 5 fps and used to evaluate the system. Players are detected and tracked at the native 25 fps with a YOLO-based tracker, while ball detection is addressed with two alternative approaches, a classical background-subtraction baseline and a pretrained deep model (WASB/HRNet), reflecting the difficulty of localizing a small, fast-moving object in wide-angle court footage. Camera calibration is used to undistort detections point-wise, and 3D player positions are recovered by triangulating matched detections across camera views, with a reprojection-consistency check and a court-plausibility filter to reject spurious cross-camera associations; this point-wise undistortion also corrects a subtle image-rectification bug -- present even in the course-provided reference script -- that we identified and fixed during development. On the annotated frames, player tracking reaches a precision of 0.70, a recall of 0.72, and an F1 score of 0.71 (mean IoU of 0.68 on matched boxes). Ball tracking proves substantially harder: under a standard IoU-based criterion both trackers essentially fail, but this conflates localization quality with box-scale mismatch, since both trackers estimate a center point rather than the ball's true extent, which itself varies sharply across cameras; under a scale-normalized, center-distance criterion the deep-learning-based tracker (F1 = 0.08) outperforms the classical baseline (F1 = 0.04) as expected, though recall remains the fundamental bottleneck. The resulting 3D reconstruction of player trajectories achieves a mean position error of 0.32 m (RMSE 0.53 m) with respect to the triangulated ground truth, over 51.6\% frame coverage. These results show that reliable player tracking and 3D localization are achievable with this camera setup, while robust ball detection remains the main open challenge.
\end{abstract}
